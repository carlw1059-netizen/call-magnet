## 1. AFCA Systemic Issues in Banking

### 1.1 Systemic Issues Insights Reports — Editions 6, 7 and 8

AFCA publishes bi-annual Systemic Issues Insights Reports summarising the systemic investigations it has completed in each six-month period. The reports are available at afca.org.au/about-afca/systemic-issues/reports. [1]

**Edition 6 (H1 FY2024–25, July–December 2024)**

Edition 6 documented systemic failures by at least one financial firm whose hardship and vulnerability policies were entirely disconnected from frontline practice. AFCA found the firm had formal written policies imposing "extra care" obligations for customers experiencing vulnerability and financial hardship, but that there were *"significant gaps between the financial firm's policies and how they were applied in practice."* [1] The root cause was inadequate training of frontline contact-centre and collections staff, who lacked the skills to identify customers experiencing vulnerability and respond appropriately.

Critically, many affected customers were in acutely vulnerable circumstances: single mothers with dependents, individuals fleeing family and domestic violence, and people experiencing homelessness. AFCA's investigation resulted in more than **$3.1 million in remediation** and relief for over **187,000 consumers**. [2]

AFCA's core finding in Edition 6: financial hardship processes must be *"accessible, responsive, and tailored to vulnerable consumers."* The gap between policy intent and operational delivery is the systemic failure pattern — not the absence of a policy, but its non-application at the point of customer contact.

**Edition 7 (H2 FY2024–25, January–June 2025)**

Edition 7 extended the picture. AFCA completed **86 in-depth systemic investigations** during this period, with systemic work benefiting more than **342,000 consumers and small businesses** and securing **$3.4 million in financial remediation**. [3]

Edition 7 identified four recurring failure patterns across banking and finance:

1. Failures in complaint handling processes
2. Gaps in recognising hardship and vulnerability at the customer contact level
3. Reliance on outdated systems unable to flag or escalate vulnerable customers
4. Misalignment between policy intent and frontline practice

This is the same policy/practice gap identified in Edition 6, now confirmed as a sector-wide pattern rather than an isolated incident. [3]

**Edition 8 (H1 FY2025–26, July–December 2025)**

Edition 8 covers AFCA's systemic investigations for the first half of FY2025–26. AFCA has confirmed its publication and noted it addresses systemic issues and "markers of excellence" for this period. Full details were not yet indexed at the time of this research. The reports page at afca.org.au/about-afca/systemic-issues/reports is the authoritative location. [1]

### 1.2 AFCA's Documented Patterns: Banks Failing Customers Who Disclose Mental Health Concerns

AFCA's published data and reports establish a consistent failure pattern specific to mental health disclosures:

**Prevalence:** In AFCA's complaint population, **6% of complainants required additional assistance**, with mental health issues being the most common reason, alongside physical impairment and family violence. [4]

**Identified failure modes:**

- *Failure to identify vulnerability at first contact:* AFCA has specifically documented that "failure to identify the customer's vulnerability resulted in the customer making repeated disclosures to multiple staff members, increasing the customer's distress in dealing with the lender." [4] Each forced re-disclosure compounds distress and represents an independent failure.

- *Inappropriate collection contact despite disclosed mental health:* AFCA found one bank continued to contact a customer directly against its own collection guidelines after the customer had disclosed mental health challenges and appointed an authorised representative. The bank also repeatedly demanded authority letters it already held. [5]

- *Inadequate adjustment of process for vulnerability:* "Vulnerable complainants are not always identified or given the care required, and sometimes they are simply not in a position to navigate the process and provide the required information in the requested format." [4] AFCA found banks applying standard hardship application processes to customers who had disclosed circumstances making those processes inaccessible.

- *Operational consequences:* AFCA has stated that "operational failures involving vulnerable consumers result in severe consequences including financial stress, mental health impacts and safety risks." [4]

**AFCA's systemic investigation findings (2024–25):** AFCA conducted systemic investigations into financial firms' compliance with both financial difficulty obligations and "extra care" obligations. Specific concerns AFCA identified during this period included:
- Failure to issue written notices when financial difficulty assistance is declined
- Inadequate processes for "extra care" of customers experiencing vulnerability
- Policies and processes "not fit to identify, and respond to, requests for help" [6]

In one investigated firm, AFCA found **228 instances of incorrect hardship decline notices** issued between 2022 and 2024. [6] This demonstrates that what looks like individual failures are often systemic.

### 1.3 CBA and AFCA Systemic Investigations

**CBA is named in a confirmed AFCA Systemic Issues Report.**

In August 2025, AFCA identified systemic issues relating to CBA and referred the matter to ASIC in a formal Systemic Issues Report. An investigation followed and concluded in October 2025. [7]

This is confirmed by CHOICE's Freedom of Information request to ASIC in early 2026. CHOICE sought access to "any Systemic Issues Reports into Commonwealth Bank's conduct and cooperation or lack thereof, in ongoing or previous AFCA cases or investigations." ASIC identified **13 documents** responsive to the request. [7]

However, ASIC blocked access to **8 of the 13 documents** — including the report itself — after consulting with CBA. ASIC's stated grounds for suppression were:

> "The release of the material would or could reasonably be expected to unreasonably affect CBA in respect of its lawful business, commercial or financial affairs." [7]

A second ground was that disclosure would deter CBA from being transparent with ASIC in future. Five heavily redacted emails between AFCA and ASIC were released; the substantive report was not. [7]

This suppression means the specific findings against CBA are not publicly available. However, the existence of the referral — AFCA identifying "systemic issues" serious enough to warrant a formal referral to ASIC — is confirmed public record.

**Additional CBA exposure:** CBA was one of the **10 large home lenders** reviewed in ASIC's Report REP 783 (*Hardship, hard to get help: Lenders fall short in financial hardship support*, 2024). ASIC's REP 783 review found that across the 10 lenders:
- More than 1 in 3 (35%) of customers dropped out of hardship application processes at least once [8]
- 40% of customers who received hardship assistance fell into arrears immediately after the assistance period ended [8]
- Lenders were failing to identify customers in financial stress, using "cookie-cutter" approaches, and imposing onerous assessment processes [8]
- ASIC Chair Joe Longo stated: "In the worst cases, lenders ignored hardship notices, effectively abandoning customers who needed their support." [8]

ASIC has directed all reviewed lenders (including CBA) to prepare action plans addressing the findings. [8]

### 1.4 Key Findings from AFCA Annual Review Pages on Financial Difficulty

**AFCA Annual Review 2024–25:**

AFCA received **100,745 complaints** in FY2024–25 — the second consecutive year above 100,000. Chief Ombudsman David Locke stated this volume is "far too high." [9]

Key financial difficulty statistics:
- Financial difficulty complaints **dropped 17%** in 2024–25 compared to 2023–24 — but AFCA explicitly cautioned this "may not indicate a sustained improvement in financial firm practices." [6]
- "Failure to respond to a request for financial difficulty assistance" **remained in the top 5 most common banking and finance complaints** for the year, with **2,890 such complaints** — barely changed from 2,838 in 2023–24. [6]
- Banking and finance complaints were 9% lower overall, but still comprised **54% of all AFCA complaints**. [6]

**AFCA Annual Review 2023 (published April 2024):**

AFCA received **5,396 complaints about financial difficulty/hardship** in 2023, a **25% increase** on the prior year. [10]

- More than **half** concerned a lender's failure to respond to a request for hardship assistance
- A third related to assistance with home loans
- AFCA stated it "had also witnessed a significant number of complaints where lenders had provided a standardised response that did not consider the customer's individual circumstances" [10]

AFCA's April 2024 statement expressed direct concern: *"AFCA is worried by rising complaints over the handling of hardship."* The regulator called out specific failures: issuing default notices to consumers in agreed repayment arrangements, placing unnecessary barriers to financial counsellors assisting consumers, and commencing debt recovery action while a matter is still before AFCA. [10]
