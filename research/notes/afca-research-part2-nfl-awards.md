## 2. AFCA Non-Financial Loss Awards in Banking

### 2.1 Compensation Caps — Complaints Lodged On or After 1 January 2024

AFCA adjusts its compensation caps every three years in line with the higher of CPI growth or Male Total Average Weekly Earnings. The current schedule, effective for all **non-superannuation complaints received on or after 1 January 2024**, is: [1]

| Type of Loss | Cap per Claim |
|---|---|
| Non-financial loss (NFL) | **$6,300** |
| Indirect financial loss | **$6,300** |
| Direct financial loss (banking/finance) | **$631,500** |
| Total monetary limit (consumer disputes) | **$1,263,000** |
| Total monetary limit (small business credit) | **$6,317,000** |

For complaints lodged between **1 January 2021 and 31 December 2023**, the NFL and indirect loss cap was $5,400.

The AFCA Rules were amended on **1 July 2024** to explicitly confirm that these limits apply **per claim**. Rule D.4 was updated to make clear that each type of loss is treated as a separate claim with its own cap ceiling. [2]

---

### 2.2 Maximum Non-Financial Loss Award and Stacking

**The $6,300 cap applies per claim — not per complaint.**

This is the most operationally significant aspect of AFCA's compensation framework for complainants with multiple bank failures. Because the Rules confirm caps operate per claim, where a bank has committed multiple distinct failures, a single complaint can generate multiple NFL awards — one per identified failure — each up to $6,300.

**The $23,900 award case:** AFCA awarded $23,900 in non-financial loss compensation in a published banking determination. The summary confirms this figure was reached through *"multiple separate failures within the one complaint."* [3] This case directly demonstrates that cumulative NFL awards across multiple bank failures within a single complaint are not subject to a single $6,300 ceiling — they aggregate.

**Stacking across heads of loss:** Beyond multiple NFL claims, a complaint can also simultaneously pursue:
- Non-financial loss (cap $6,300 per failure)
- Indirect financial loss (cap $6,300 per claim)
- Direct financial loss (cap $631,500 per claim)

Each head has its own cap. Total compensation can therefore substantially exceed any individual cap. A complainant with three distinct NFL failures, indirect financial loss, and quantifiable direct financial loss could potentially receive:
- 3 × NFL up to $6,300 = up to $18,900
- Indirect financial loss = up to $6,300
- Direct financial loss = up to $631,500

AFCA's Operational Guidelines set out how these are assessed. The key requirement is that each head of loss is pleaded with specificity and causation is established for each. [4]

**Consumer advocacy context:** Consumer Action Law Centre's 2023 submission on AFCA's non-financial loss approach criticised AFCA for "chronically underassessing the impact of financial firms' conduct on complainants." Consumer Action argued the case studies AFCA chose to illustrate the approach showed too little weight given to the real-world harm caused by bank failures. This is the adversarial backdrop: complainants and their advocates consider AFCA's baseline NFL awards conservative. [5]

### 2.3 The Non-Financial Loss Matrix: Impact × Duration

AFCA's published approach to NFL claims uses a matrix that weighs the **impact** of the firm's conduct against the **duration** of that impact to calibrate the award amount. [6]

**Impact axis:**
- Nil — no discernible effect on complainant
- Low — minor inconvenience
- Medium — significant disruption, stress or inconvenience beyond what is ordinary
- High — severe distress, health impact, major interference with complainant's life or peace of mind

**Duration axis:**
- Short — weeks
- Medium — months
- Long — an extended period (many months to over a year)

**High Impact / Long Duration intersection:** This cell produces awards at or approaching the maximum cap — for pre-2024 complaints, $5,400; for post-1 January 2024 complaints, **$6,300**. [6]

For a complainant to reach the high/long cell, AFCA must find:

1. The bank's conduct caused *severe* distress or interference (not merely ordinary inconvenience — AFCA explicitly requires the complainant to be "moderately robust") [7]
2. That impact persisted over an extended period

Mental health disclosures are directly relevant to the impact axis. A complainant who disclosed mental health vulnerability at the outset, whose disclosure was ignored, who was forced to re-disclose repeatedly, who continued to receive inappropriate contact from collections staff, and who experienced the consequent exacerbation of their mental health condition over many months — this is precisely the factual matrix that positions a case in the High/Long cell.

**AFCA's stated grounds for NFL in banking:**

From published determinations, AFCA awards NFL in banking where the firm's conduct caused:
- An unusual degree or extent of physical inconvenience
- Unusual time taken to resolve a situation
- Interference with the complainant's expectation of enjoyment or peace of mind
- Significant stress and anxiety beyond the ordinary level involved in any complaint

The critical qualifier is *"unusual"* — it must exceed what a moderately robust person would ordinarily experience in the course of a legitimate dispute. Where a bank's conduct involves repeated failures over an extended period against a customer who has disclosed vulnerability, the "ordinary" baseline is already elevated, and AFCA's threshold for what is "unusual" is easier to cross.

### 2.4 Mental Health Distress as a Factor in Award Amounts — Case Examples

**Case with mental health disclosure and Banking Code breach:**

A published AFCA determination involved a complainant who disclosed mental health challenges to their bank. Despite this disclosure and the appointment of an authorised representative:
- The bank continued to contact the complainant directly, contrary to its own collection guidelines
- The bank repeatedly requested authority letters it already held on file
- The bank incorrectly recorded adverse repayment history against the complainant

AFCA found the bank's conduct did not align with its Banking Code of Practice obligations, particularly the provisions requiring support for vulnerable customers. AFCA awarded **$6,700 in non-financial loss compensation** for the stress and inconvenience caused. [3] (Note: this award exceeds the $5,400 cap applicable before 1 January 2024 — under the current $6,300 cap, the award would be within cap.)

The award was characterised as *"appropriate compensation for the stress that the complainant has suffered from the bank's conduct."*

**The $23,900 aggregate case:**

In a banking determination involving multiple separate failures within one complaint, AFCA awarded $23,900 in total non-financial loss. This figure results from stacking individual NFL awards for each distinct bank failure — not from a single high award. [3] This is the ceiling-breaking mechanism: not a single exceptional award, but multiple distinct failures each reaching or approaching the per-claim cap.

**AFCA's framing of mental health relevance:**

AFCA's approach documents and determinations confirm that:
- A complainant's disclosed mental health condition is directly relevant to the impact axis of the NFL matrix
- Where the bank knew of the condition and failed to adjust its conduct accordingly, the bank's culpability for the impact is heightened
- Ongoing contact by collection staff despite a mental health disclosure — particularly where an authorised representative has been appointed — constitutes a distinct failure capable of generating its own NFL award

---

### 2.5 Limits on AFCA's Non-Financial Loss Compensation

AFCA's approach is deliberately conservative. The authority explicitly states it expects "claimants to be moderately robust and will not award compensation for the ordinary degree of inconvenience involved in making a claim." [7]

There are three practical limits that complainants should understand:

1. **Each NFL claim requires independent causation.** For each separate bank failure cited, the complainant must establish that the failure *caused* the distress or inconvenience alleged — not merely that distress occurred during the period.

2. **The $6,300 cap per claim is a ceiling, not a target.** AFCA has discretion to award less than the cap even at the High/Long intersection. The $23,900 case represents an exceptional outcome from multiple compounding failures — it is not routinely achieved.

3. **The "moderately robust" standard.** Claimants with known mental health conditions face a double-edged sword: the condition makes them more susceptible to harm (supporting higher impact rating) but AFCA still applies the moderately robust baseline to the initial threshold question.
